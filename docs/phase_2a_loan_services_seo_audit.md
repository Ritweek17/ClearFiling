# Phase 2A — Loan Services SEO Content Audit

**Audit scope:** 20 indexable loan-service routes in the current service registry\
**Branch:** `seo/phase-2a-loan-services-audit`\
**Base:** verified GitHub `main`, `93cd463a19942399ba39563b7c42a0c5f0d702b5`\
**Audit type:** source review and content planning; no website copy or SEO configuration changed.

## Executive summary

The authoritative `INDEXABLE_SERVICE_SLUGS` list contains 23 routes. Removing the three corporate services (`private-limited-company`, `gst-registration`, and `trademark-registration`) leaves exactly 20 loan routes. Every one is in the `Loan Services` category and has `indexStatus: 'index'` in `SERVICE_REGISTRY`; none of the three corporate pages is included in this loan audit.

All 20 loan records lack custom `metaTitle` and `metaDescription`. The current title is therefore the generic fallback `${service.title} | ClearFiling`; the current description is the record’s `description`. Titles are short and brand-only. Description lengths range from 88 to 162 characters, and several make broad claims without context. Every page has a basic H1, four benefit bullets, three to five document bullets, and one FAQ (Business Loan has two). None has `longDescription`, `eligibility`, or `process`; 19 have no related-service links. The shared template provides useful mobile-responsive presentation and a lead consultation CTA, but these short records do not give users enough lender-neutral detail to make an informed decision.

Priority findings:

- **High — unsupported financial/product claims:** rates, funding percentages, approval speed, disbursement time, product eligibility, repayment terms, tax treatment, government-scheme benefits, lender relationships, and service turnaround are asserted in the records. The codebase provides no evidence that ClearFiling is a lender or that these statements are approved/current. Flag for owner and qualified financial/tax review before rewriting or promoting them.
- **High — role clarity:** page copy sometimes reads as though ClearFiling itself provides or controls credit. Clarify, after owner confirmation, that lenders set terms and make credit decisions and state precisely what ClearFiling does. Do not imply guaranteed sanction.
- **High — shallow content:** no page has eligibility/process/long-form content, and 19 omit related links. The pages are mostly a templated hero, generic benefit cards, document list, one FAQ, and a CTA.
- **Medium — metadata:** titles have no distinct query modifier; all use the same pattern. Descriptions are often short, generic, or claim-heavy. Draft alternatives are in the content plan and require owner approval.
- **Medium — FAQ coverage and mobile legibility:** 19 pages have one FAQ. Answers include unsupported, categorical claims. FAQ text renders at small sizes on mobile; visual device testing was not part of this source audit.

No change to robots, canonical URLs, sitemap membership, indexability, routes, claims, or visible content was made. No content claim was independently verified against a lender, regulator, scheme administrator, tax authority, or ClearFiling owner. The “unsupported” label means the repository alone does not substantiate the claim.

## Cross-page findings

### Metadata and search intent

- **Titles:** every title uses the same fallback pattern and includes only service label + brand. Title length is 22–37 characters; a unique, useful intent modifier is absent. Add a concise modifier (application guidance, documents, business use, or asset type) only after confirming ClearFiling’s actual scope.
- **Descriptions:** all 20 are derived from the service `description` field, not a custom SEO field. Lengths range 88–162; short descriptions vary between broader marketing copy and application support. Avoid composing snippets that assert loan availability, rates, eligibility, approval or speed. The content plan supplies differentiated drafts for owner review.
- **H1/heading hierarchy:** active loan pages render one H1 (`heroTitle || title`), then shared H2 sections such as “Overview of [service] Services,” benefits, documents, FAQ, and related services where configured. H3s label benefit/document cards. No page has a loan-specific eligibility or process section because the data fields are absent. Some page headings (e.g. shared “Registration Process Flow”) are not loan-appropriate, though the section is conditional and not rendered for these 20 records.
- **Intent overlap:** Vehicle Loan vs Car/Bike/Commercial Vehicle; Business vs MSME/Startup/Working Capital; Working Capital vs Cash Credit/Overdraft; Machinery vs Equipment vs Project vs Construction; and Loan Against Property vs Business Loan need explicit scope boundaries and cross-links.

### Content usefulness, duplication, and trust

- Each page has four generic-looking benefit cards and a short checklist, but lacks a useful explainer for how a reader evaluates a lender product, what information varies by lender, common application stages, costs/risks to compare, or what ClearFiling actually does. Adding words alone would not solve the trust issue; content should answer the intent with sourced, appropriately qualified information.
- Every page uses the same page skeleton and CTA (“Request Free Consultation”); only Business Loan currently has related-service links. Template repetition is expected, but the copy is thin and many benefit claims share “quick,” “flexible,” “competitive/attractive,” “customized,” and “high-value” language. Keep page-specific examples, but do not manufacture differentiators.
- There are 20 FAQs in total across the 20 routes, but 19 pages have only one each. Existing questions are route-specific at a basic level, yet several answers assert universal lender terms or tax/scheme rules. Add reader questions on lender decision-making, fees, collateral/security, documents, repayment, application status, and what ClearFiling does; cite current primary sources and avoid individualized financial/tax advice.
- Trust signals should explain the advisory/documentation role, disclose that the lender controls product terms and decisions, show only verified professional qualifications/relationships, and date/source scheme or tax information. No lender logos, credentials, lender partnerships, or approval statistics should be added without documentation and owner approval.

### Repeated claims requiring review

The following assertions recur or carry high consumer impact. The repository does not substantiate them:

- **Rates/fees:** “competitive/attractive/preferential” rates; subsidized rates and processing fees; no prepayment charges.
- **Amounts/tenures:** 80%, 90%, 95%, or 100% funding; up to 30, 15, 7, 5, or 4 years; “high-value”/high limits.
- **Approval/disbursement/process timing:** multiple “quick/instant/super fast” approval claims, approval within hours, under-60-minute disbursal, timeline badges from one to 30 business days, and “fast disbursal.” It is unclear whether displayed `processingTime` means ClearFiling’s work or a lender’s credit decision/disbursement.
- **Product/eligibility universals:** no collateral, no income/credit checks, fixed credit score preference, required Udyam subsidy entitlement, eligible property/vehicle categories, and clean OD availability.
- **Benefits/tax/schemes:** Home Loan deductions, Section 80E, MSME subsidies/CGTMSE/PMEGP/Mudra benefits, startup grants, and depreciation claims require current specialist review and primary-source citations.
- **Capability/relationships:** “leading banks,” dealer ties/offers, bank coordination, CA-prepared project reports, LC/trade finance facilitation, equipment lease advisory, secure insured gold storage, and end-to-end tracking need business-owner evidence.
- **Service commitments:** free consultation and advisors calling “shortly” appear in the shared component. Confirm the consultation is free and response-time promise can be met. Keep these separate from lender timelines.

## Structured data and technical presentation

`ServiceDetail.jsx` emits Service, BreadcrumbList and FAQPage JSON-LD through the existing schema helpers. The FAQ schema mirrors the visible FAQ record; accuracy problems in an answer therefore also appear in structured data. The Service schema currently labels the provider as the organization and supplies a service URL, service name, description and India service area. It also includes a generic `Offer` with currency and availability but no price. Audit whether this represents a real offer for credit arranged by an advisory firm; do not imply ClearFiling lends or makes an offer of credit. Schema correction is outside this content-only phase and would require a separate approved technical change.

Mobile classes stack the hero and use responsive grids (`grid-cols-1`, `sm:`, `md:`, `lg:`) with wrapping CTA buttons. This is a reasonable source-level responsive structure, not a visual/device acceptance test. FAQ answers use `text-xs sm:text-sm` and should be checked at narrow widths for readable type and accordion interaction. No mobile screenshot or live accessibility test was performed.

## Recommended implementation sequence

1. Obtain owner confirmation of ClearFiling’s exact loan-related role, service coverage, free-consultation promise, response expectations, and any actual lender/partner relationships.
2. Build a claim ledger for all 20 pages (rates, amounts, durations, eligibility, schemes, tax, processing, approvals, custody, qualifications); collect dated primary sources and remove or qualify anything unsupported in a later approved content change.
3. Have a qualified financial/tax reviewer check scheme, lending, tax, secured-asset, and eligibility explanations. Separate general education from commitments made by ClearFiling.
4. Approve page-specific search intent, scope boundaries, H2 outline, FAQ questions/answers, and cross-link targets using `docs/phase_2a_loan_services_content_plan.md`.
5. Implement metadata/content in a small group of related pages, keeping all service slugs, canonical URLs, `noindex` rules, sitemap membership, redirects, design system, and schema behavior unchanged unless separately authorized.
6. Add content/metadata regression tests, inspect generated HTML and JSON-LD, compare sitemap and robots hashes, and review narrow mobile layouts before extending the batch.
